## 1. Executive Summary

This document outlines Phorma's strategy to secure revenue and partnerships through the "Apoyo a la Investigación Científica" (AIC) grant. The call offers up to **$200,000 USD** (80% non-reimbursable) to public/private non-profit research institutions. The core objective is to **promote basic research that strengthens value chains and technological capabilities**, fostering collaboration with the private sector.

**Phorma's Opportunity:** As a Research Software Engineering (RSE) and SciML consultancy, Phorma is uniquely positioned to be the "technological enabler" that allows research groups to meet the grant's requirement of industrial transferability. We can be hired as a specialized service provider to transform academic prototypes into production-ready systems.

## 2. Alignment with Phorma's Mission

*   **AIC Goal:** "Strengthen value chains and technological capabilities."
*   **Phorma's Mission:** "Transform research prototypes into maintainable, tested, and scalable software systems." (from *Mission: The Gap & The Bridge*)

The grant explicitly seeks to bridge the gap between basic science and application. Phorma *is* that bridge.

## 3. Primary Revenue Avenue: Direct Service Provider

Research groups applying for AIC need to demonstrate how they will execute complex computational tasks or deliver usable technology.

*   **The Model:** Phorma acts as a specialized vendor providing "Servicios Técnicos Especializados" (Specialized Technical Services).
*   **The Pitch:** We take the engineering burden off the research team. Our involvement de-risks the "technological capability" section of the proposal, making it more attractive to evaluators.
*   **Value Add:** We ensure that the final deliverable is a production-ready, reproducible system that facilitates industrial transfer, rather than just a collection of research scripts.

**Critical Caveat:** We must verify if the grant permits a "Service Provider" to consume a significant portion of the budget or if it prioritizes "Adopters" (partners who use/invest in the result).

## 7. Risk Mitigation (Updated via Rule of 5)

### Financial & Legal Risks (CRITICAL)
*   **Payment Latency & Inflation:** State funding often operates on reimbursement (Rendición de Cuentas).
    *   *Mitigation:* If "Advance" is not possible, factor financial cost into the "Bronze/Silver/Gold" pricing. Quote in USD-equivalent units if legally permissible, or overestimate ARS costs to buffer inflation.
*   **Exchange Rate Gap:** The grant is likely paid at the Official ARS rate, while real costs may track MEP/Blue.
    *   *Mitigation:* Calculate the "Real Value" of the contract at the free market rate to ensure profitability before signing.
*   **Budget Caps:** If "Services" are capped at <20%, we may need to structure Phorma as a "co-beneficiary" rather than a vendor (more complex).

### Operational & Reputation Risks
*   **Scope Creep:** "Production-ready" is subjective.
    *   *Mitigation:* Define "Done" rigorously in the Partner Kit (e.g., "Passes CI/CD pipeline," not "Experiment works").
*   **Reputational Contagion:** If the PI's science fails, the software is useless.
    *   *Mitigation:* Contractual clauses separating "Software Integrity" from "Scientific Outcome."
*   **Bureaucratic Gridlock:** University admins blocking external vendors.
    *   *Mitigation:* Engage UVT admins early; prefer UVTs known for agility (e.g., private foundations linked to universities).

---
**Conclusion:** The AIC call is a strategic catalyst, but carries significant financial risk due to local economic dynamics. We proceed **only if** the "Critical Viability Checks" (Phase 1) confirm a sustainable payment model.
